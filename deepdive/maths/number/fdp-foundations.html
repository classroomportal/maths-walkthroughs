Deep Dive: Fractions, Decimals & Percentages — Part A

Foundations, equivalence, and finding a percentage of an amount

Before You Start

Most students: around 25–35 minutes.
Still building confidence with this topic? Take your time — up to 45–50 minutes is completely fine, there's no rush.
Already confident? You might move through it in 15–20 minutes.
Calculator or no calculator?
Both come up in exams, so this walkthrough covers both. Stage 6 (multiplier method) is the fast route when you do have a calculator. Stage 7 (building blocks) is the method to know cold for non-calculator papers — it only needs the times tables and halving, done in your head or on paper.
Stage 1

What is a Fraction?

Key words
Fraction — a part of a whole, written as one number over another.
Numerator — the top number: how many parts you have.
Denominator — the bottom number: how many equal parts the whole is split into.

Imagine a chocolate bar split into equal pieces. If it's split into 5 equal pieces and you eat 3 of them, you've eaten 35 of the bar.

3 out of 5 equal parts shaded = 35

Stage 2

What is a Decimal?

Key words
Decimal point — separates whole numbers from parts of a whole.
Tenths — the first digit after the decimal point (out of 10 equal parts).
Hundredths — the second digit after the point (out of 100 equal parts).

A decimal is just another way of writing a fraction with a denominator of 10, 100, 1000, and so on. The digit straight after the point tells you tenths; the next digit tells you hundredths.

37 out of 100 small squares shaded = 0.37 = 37100

Stage 3

What is a Percentage?

Key words
Per cent — literally means "out of a hundred" (Latin: per centum).
% — the percentage symbol.

A percentage is exactly the same hundred-grid idea as Stage 2 — it's just a different way of writing it. 37 shaded squares out of 100 is 0.37 and 37%. To turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100 (and add the % sign). To go back, divide by 100.

0.37 × 100 = 37, so this grid is 37%

Stage 4

The Conversion Triangle

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three names for the same amount. You can convert between any two of them, in either direction:

No calculator? For "friendly" denominators (2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100) you can do fraction → decimal by short division, in your head or on paper — you don't need a calculator for 3 ÷ 4. A calculator just saves time, it doesn't unlock anything new here.
Worked example: Convert 34 to a decimal and a percentage.
Fraction → decimal: 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
Decimal → percentage: 0.75 × 100 = 75%
Convert 40% back to a fraction.
40% → decimal: 40 ÷ 100 = 0.4
Decimal → fraction: 0.4 = 410 = 25 (simplified)
Stage 5

Ordering a Mixed Set

When a question mixes fractions, decimals and percentages together, the safest approach is to convert everything into the same form first — decimals are usually quickest to compare.

Worked example: Order 34, 0.7, and 68% from smallest to largest.
Convert all to decimals: 34 = 0.75, 0.7 stays 0.7, 68% = 0.68
Order: 0.68 < 0.7 < 0.75, so: 68% < 0.7 < 34
Stage 6

Percentage of an Amount — Multiplier Method Calculator

To find a percentage of an amount, convert the percentage to a decimal (its multiplier) and multiply.

Worked example: Find 35% of £160.
35% → decimal multiplier: 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35
0.35 × 160 = £56
Remember: "of" means "×" in maths. So "35% of 160" literally means 0.35 × 160. This is exactly the same skill as Stage 4's fraction-to-decimal conversion, just used to scale an amount.
Stage 7

Percentage of an Amount — Building-Block Method Non-Calculator

Without a calculator, it's often faster to build the percentage you need out of 10%, 5%, and 1% blocks.

Worked example: Find 35% of £160 (no calculator).
10% of 160 = 16
So 30% of 160 = 16 × 3 = 48
5% of 160 = 16 ÷ 2 = 8
35% = 30% + 5% = 48 + 8 = £56 ✓ (matches Stage 6's answer)
Find 15% of £80.
10% of 80 = 8
5% of 80 = 4
15% = 10% + 5% = 8 + 4 = £12
